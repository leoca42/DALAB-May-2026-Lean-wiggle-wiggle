Perturbation Dictionary

negate
Negates the entire statement (i.e., applies ¬ to the conclusion). If the original theorem is True, the negated version is mathematically False.
contrapose
Creates the contrapositive of an implication (P → Q becomes ¬Q → ¬P). The resulting statement is logically equivalent and maintains its True status.
converse
Swaps the hypothesis and conclusion of an implication (P → Q becomes Q → P). The mathematical truth of the resulting statement is generally unknown without a new proof.
generalize
Removes unnecessary or unused hypotheses from the statement. Because the conclusion did not strictly rely on them, the statement remains True.
tc_generalize
Weakens a typeclass in the hypothesis to a parent class (e.g., generalizing from a Commutative Ring to just a Ring). The statement remains True if the proof did not rely on the stricter class properties.
tc_weaken_hyp
Strengthens a typeclass in the hypothesis to a child class (e.g., from Monoid to Commutative Monoid). By giving the hypothesis more structure than it strictly needs, the statement naturally remains True.
tc_strengthen_conc
Strengthens the required typeclass of the conclusion. Because it is demanding a stronger result from the same hypotheses, the truth status is unknown.
tc_weaken_conc
Weakens the required typeclass of the conclusion. Because it is demanding less than the original proof provided, the statement remains True.
